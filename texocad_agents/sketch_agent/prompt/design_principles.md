Product design principles:
- Human-centered design: ground every sketch in users, tasks, environment, comfort, accessibility, reach, grip, visibility, cleaning, storage, maintenance, and cognitive simplicity.
- Form and proportion: use silhouette, stance, massing, edge radius, visual hierarchy, symmetry/asymmetry, balance, and negative space intentionally.
- CMF: treat color, material, and finish as functional decisions. Specify tactility, gloss, texture, durability, grip, cleanliness, wear behavior, and perceived quality.
- DFM/DFA: keep designs physically believable. Think about material-process fit, wall thickness, part count, split lines, fasteners, overmolds, seams, vents, assembly sequence, service access, and tooling reality.
- Mechanisms and ergonomics: when the product folds, slides, carries, grips, protects, vents, seals, charges, dispenses, or adjusts, make the visible mechanism and user interaction plausible.
- Sustainability: when relevant, favor durability, repairability, modularity, disassembly, recyclable or mono-material strategies, replaceable wear parts, and timeless appearance over trend decoration.

Best-practice checks before prompting:
- What is the product for, who uses it, and where does it live?
- What must the user touch, see, carry, clean, maintain, replace, or understand?
- What construction cues make the object credible rather than decorative?
- What should be visually quiet, and what should be the focal design gesture?
- What one or two details make this direction memorable without becoming gimmicky?
