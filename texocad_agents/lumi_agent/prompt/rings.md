Ring design reference:

Ring anatomy:
- Shank or band: the circular part around the finger. Can be straight, tapered, split, knife-edge, rounded, flat, domed, comfort-fit, twisted, bypass, open, or cigar-width.
- Head: the top structure that holds the main stone.
- Basket: the under-stone structure beneath prongs; can be airy, cathedral, floral, geometric, low-profile, or architectural.
- Gallery: the visible side detail under the center stone, often used for hidden halos, scrollwork, filigree, or support bars.
- Shoulders: the band area leading into the center stone; can be plain, pavé-set, channel-set, twisted, split, tapered, or accented.
- Bridge: the underside connection across the finger beneath the head.
- Prongs: small claws holding a stone. Common styles include round, claw, petite claw, double claw, tab, compass, V-prong, and shared prong.
- Bezel: a rim of metal around a stone; can be full, half, scalloped, milgrain, or low modern.
- Halo: smaller stones surrounding a center stone. Can be single, double, hidden, floral, scalloped, cushion halo around round stone, or geometric.

Core ring categories:
- Solitaire: one main stone, clean and timeless.
- Three-stone: center stone with two side stones, often symbolic and balanced.
- Five-stone or anniversary band: repeated stones across the top.
- Eternity or half-eternity: stones around all or part of the band.
- Signet: broad face, usually metal-led, engraved, gemstone-inlaid, or sculptural.
- Cluster: multiple stones grouped into one focal form.
- Cocktail: larger statement ring with dramatic scale and color.
- Toi et moi: two main stones, often contrasting cuts or colors.
- Bypass: band ends curve past each other, good for motion and asymmetry.
- Stackable: slim rings designed to layer.
- Contour or nesting band: shaped to fit around another ring.

Stone shapes:
- Round brilliant: classic sparkle, balanced, versatile.
- Oval: elongated, graceful, flattering, often romantic.
- Emerald cut: step-cut, architectural, elegant, less sparkly but very clear and refined.
- Asscher: square step-cut, Art Deco, geometric.
- Cushion: soft square or rectangle, romantic, vintage-friendly.
- Pear: teardrop shape, directional, delicate or dramatic.
- Marquise: pointed oval, elongated, regal, vintage or bold.
- Princess: square brilliant, crisp and contemporary.
- Radiant: rectangular or square brilliant, bright with clipped corners.
- Baguette: slim step-cut accent, linear and architectural.
- Trillion: triangular, sharp, modern side-stone or center-stone option.
- Heart: symbolic and expressive; works best when the user explicitly wants sentimentality.
- Rose cut: flat-backed dome facets, antique, soft glow, low profile.
- Cabochon: smooth domed stone, tactile, ancient, sculptural.
- Freeform or portrait cut: artistic, bespoke, often modern or heirloom-feeling.

Gem and stone families:
- Diamond: white, champagne, salt-and-pepper, black, cognac, gray, or fancy color.
- Sapphire: blue, parti, teal, green, yellow, pink, peach, white; strong everyday option.
- Ruby: vivid red to pinkish red, classic and saturated.
- Emerald: rich green, often included, better with protective settings.
- Morganite: peach-pink, soft romantic color, less durable for hard daily wear.
- Aquamarine: pale blue, clean and calm.
- Spinel: durable, bright, available in red, pink, lavender, gray, blue, and black.
- Moissanite: bright, high fire, diamond alternative.
- Garnet: red, rhodolite, tsavorite green, demantoid green, spessartine orange.
- Amethyst: purple, accessible, not ideal for rough daily wear.
- Topaz: blue, white, imperial tones; can be large and clear.
- Tourmaline: watermelon, pink, green, teal, indicolite, chrome; expressive color range.
- Opal: luminous and fragile, best protected and not for rough everyday wear.
- Pearl: soft, organic, delicate, sculptural, needs protective design.
- Moonstone: glowing, soft, ethereal, better for occasional wear.
- Onyx, lapis, turquoise, malachite, jade: opaque color stones for signet, inlay, cabochon, and bold graphic designs.

Settings:
- Prong setting: maximizes light and delicacy; more exposed.
- Bezel setting: protective, modern, smooth, good for active wear.
- Tension-inspired setting: sleek floating look; use visually, but avoid claiming true engineering feasibility.
- Cathedral setting: shoulders rise to support the head; elegant and traditional.
- Basket setting: visible side architecture under stone; versatile.
- Flush or gypsy setting: stone sits level in metal; clean and durable.
- Channel setting: stones sit between metal walls; orderly and smooth.
- Pavé setting: tiny stones close together; delicate sparkle.
- Micro-pavé: very fine sparkle; visually luxurious but intricate.
- Burnish setting: stone pressed into metal; subtle and durable-looking.
- Bar setting: stones separated by metal bars; graphic.
- Milgrain: tiny beaded metal edge; vintage detail.
- Filigree: open metal lacework; antique and ornate.
- Engraving: hand-cut or pattern detail on metal; personal and tactile.

Metals and finishes:
- Platinum: cool white, dense, premium, durable feel.
- White gold: cool white, polished, classic; may imply rhodium-plated brightness.
- Yellow gold: warm, classic, vintage or modern depending on form.
- Rose gold: pink warmth, romantic and soft.
- Champagne gold or green gold: nuanced warm tones for bespoke looks.
- Sterling silver: bright and accessible; more casual.
- Blackened metal, oxidized silver, black rhodium: dramatic contrast.
- Two-tone metal: useful for contrast, e.g. yellow gold band with platinum head.
- Finishes: high polish, satin, brushed, matte, hammered, sandblasted, Florentine, engraved, milgrain, bark texture, organic molten texture.

Style vocabulary:
- Minimal: plain shank, simple setting, clean proportions.
- Modern: bezel, low profile, sculptural geometry, negative space.
- Classic: solitaire, cathedral, pavé shoulders, round or oval diamond.
- Vintage: milgrain, filigree, engraving, halo, rose cut, old mine or old European feel.
- Art Deco: emerald or Asscher cuts, baguettes, stepped geometry, symmetry.
- Organic: asymmetry, vine forms, pebble settings, leaf or floral details.
- Celestial: star-set stones, crescent curves, scattered diamonds.
- Romantic: oval, pear, cushion, rose gold, delicate pavé, soft shoulders.
- Bold: cocktail scale, signet face, wide band, high contrast stones.
- Heirloom: refined proportions, meaningful details, timeless materials.

Practical design guidance:
- Everyday rings usually benefit from lower profiles, protected edges, smoother settings, and durable stones.
- Engagement-style rings need a clear center-stone hierarchy and wearable proportions.
- Very high settings show the stone but can snag.
- Very thin bands look delicate but can appear fragile; balance delicacy with visual support.
- Soft or porous stones are better in bezels, signets, cabochons, or occasional-wear designs.
- Pointed stone corners, such as marquise, pear, and princess, often look safer with V-prongs or bezels.
- East-west orientation makes elongated stones more modern and lower-looking.
- North-south orientation feels classic and lengthens the finger.
- Wider bands can make smaller stones feel intentional.
- A hidden halo adds sparkle from side views without changing the top silhouette.
- Matching wedding bands need enough clearance or a contour/nesting shape.

Useful default directions:
- If the user wants timeless: oval or round solitaire, platinum or yellow gold, tapered band, low basket or cathedral.
- If the user wants modern: bezel-set emerald cut or oval, satin gold, low profile, plain or wide shank.
- If the user wants vintage: cushion or old mine style, milgrain bezel or halo, engraved shoulders, warm yellow gold.
- If the user wants delicate: petite oval, slim rounded band, tiny pavé shoulders, low basket.
- If the user wants bold: wide signet or cocktail ring, cabochon or emerald-cut color stone, sculptural shoulders.
- If the user wants unusual but wearable: east-west oval bezel, teal sapphire, brushed yellow gold, softly tapered band.
