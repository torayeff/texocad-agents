Required fields for a useful first concept:

- Ring category.
- Wear frequency.
- Style preference.
- Metal color or metal type.
- Centre stone preference, or explicit no-stone direction.
- Stone shape preference if a centre stone is wanted.
- Setting preference or priority: sparkle, protection, low profile, symbolism, or statement scale.
- Budget sensitivity or practicality level.

Helpful optional fields:

- Ring size.
- Desired band width.
- Preferred profile height.
- Wedding-band or stack compatibility.
- Accent stone preference.
- Finish preference.
- Inspiration references.
- Finger or hand proportions.
- Skin sensitivity.
- Existing stones to reuse.
- Cultural, symbolic, memorial, or heirloom details.

When critical information is missing, ask no more than two or three questions at once. Good first questions are: intended use, metal color, and centre stone or setting preference.

## Visual Description Prompt

Describe a ring with this structure:

1. Overall family: solitaire, halo, signet, band, cocktail, eternity, cluster, toi et moi.
2. Top-view silhouette: round, oval, elongated, wide, split, bypass, geometric, organic.
3. Side profile: low, medium, high, cathedral, basket, open gallery, closed gallery.
4. Stone program: centre shape, side stones, accent stones, color, layout.
5. Setting method: prong, bezel, channel, pave, flush, tension, bead, bar.
6. Shank: width, taper, cross-section, comfort fit, euro, knife edge, split.
7. Decorative details: milgrain, engraving, filigree, texture, inlay, enamel.
8. Manufacturing intent: daily durable, delicate occasion, castable, resizable, stackable.

Example:

"A low-profile oval sapphire bezel ring with a 2.2 mm comfort-fit yellow gold shank, slightly tapered shoulders, open side gallery for cleaning, two flush-set diamond accents on each shoulder, satin finish on the shank, and a plain lower sizing area."

## Image Generation Prompt Template

Use this structure when the output is meant for a visual model:

```text
A [ring category] featuring a [centre stone shape] [stone type] in a [setting type] setting, with a [band style] band in [metal type/color]. The design style is [style tags]. The ring has a [low/medium/high] profile, [finish type] finish, and [accent stone details if any]. Show accurate jewellery proportions, realistic metal reflections, secure stone setting, clear top-view silhouette, and a three-quarter view that shows side profile and gallery construction.
```

Negative prompt ideas:

```text
Avoid unrealistic floating stones, unsupported prongs, overly thin metal, distorted gemstone facets, extra stones not requested, fantasy materials, bulky proportions, incorrect ring anatomy, impossible tension settings, and exaggerated reflections.
```

## Daily Wear Design Checklist

- Stone is hard and tough enough for expected wear.
- Soft or brittle stones are bezel-set or otherwise protected.
- Setting is low enough to avoid constant snagging.
- Prongs do not protrude beyond stone outline.
- Pointed stone tips have V-prongs or bezel protection.
- Shank is not too thin for the stone mass.
- Lower shank has enough thickness for long-term wear.
- Inside edges are comfort-rounded.
- Top weight is controlled to reduce spinning.
- Design can be cleaned with realistic access under stones.
- Maintenance burden matches the wearer.
- Resizing needs are addressed.

## Engagement Ring Checklist

- Centre stone shape complements the chosen setting.
- Stone height balances brilliance, snagging, and wedding-band fit.
- Gallery looks intentional from side view.
- Wedding band pairing is decided: flush, contour, nesting, or intentional gap.
- Shank width visually balances centre stone size.
- Shoulders provide enough support for the head.
- Prong count and shape suit stone size and outline.
- Accent stones do not weaken the shank or sizing area.
- Hidden details do not compromise cleaning or strength.
- Future resizing is possible unless explicitly rejected.

## Wedding Band Checklist

- Width matches comfort expectations.
- Interior profile is chosen intentionally, especially for wide bands.
- Band size was checked with similar-width sizer if possible.
- Edges are softened.
- If stacking, total stack width is considered.
- Surface pattern will survive wear.
- Stones are protected from palm-side abrasion.
- Full eternity resizability limitation is accepted.
- Finish is realistic for daily contact.
- Pairing with engagement ring avoids destructive rubbing.

## Signet Ring Checklist

- Face size suits finger scale.
- Face thickness supports engraving or stone inlay.
- Shoulders transition smoothly into shank.
- Lower shank is comfortable and not overly heavy.
- Top does not rotate excessively.
- Engraving style is chosen: monogram, crest, seal, relief, intaglio.
- Any inlay material is compatible with wear and repair expectations.
- Flat planes are slightly softened for comfort.

## Cocktail Or Statement Ring Checklist

- Intended as occasional wear unless engineered otherwise.
- Large stones have enough protection.
- High profile is stable on the finger.
- Shank is wide or balanced enough to carry top weight.
- Undergallery is comfortable and clears the finger.
- Fragile stones are placed away from exposed edges.
- Cleaning access exists around cluster stones.
- The ring can be put on and removed without catching adjacent fingers.

## CAD Review Checklist

- Ring size is correct.
- Units are correct.
- Mesh or solid is watertight.
- No naked edges, self-intersections, or accidental internal faces.
- Minimum wall thickness is appropriate for metal and process.
- Shank thickness is appropriate for daily wear.
- Openings are large enough to cast and clean.
- Stone seats are dimensioned from actual stone measurements when possible.
- Pavilion and culet clear gallery and finger.
- Prongs overlap girdles and have enough metal after seat cutting.
- Bezels have enough wall thickness and height.
- Channel walls have enough height and thickness.
- Pave areas have enough metal between stones.
- Fine details have enough depth to survive polishing.
- Sharp comfort edges are softened.
- Lower sizing area is preserved if needed.
- Hollow areas have drain/vent strategy if printed.
- Sprue/support cleanup will not destroy visible details.

## Manufacturability Red Flags

- "Make it as thin as possible" for a daily ring.
- Large centre stone on sub-1.5 mm shank with no support.
- Full pave all the way around but user expects resizing.
- Opal, pearl, turquoise, or emerald in high exposed prongs for everyday wear.
- Tension setting requested in soft metal or with fragile stone.
- Deep tiny lettering or holes in cast design.
- Gallery so closed that cleaning is impossible.
- Gallery so open that stone support is weak.
- Prongs modeled too thin before polishing.
- Stone seats modeled final-size with no setter allowance.
- Ring designed only from top view with no side-view resolution.
- Wedding band flush fit promised without checking basket/head geometry.

## Setting Choice Matrix

- Maximum sparkle: prong, halo, open gallery.
- Maximum protection: bezel, flush, channel.
- Low snag: bezel, flush, low channel, low dome.
- Vintage detail: milgrain, bead setting, bright cut, filigree, engraving.
- Modern minimal: full bezel, east-west stone, smooth shank, matte finish.
- Active lifestyle: bezel, channel, flush, sturdy low prongs.
- Fragile stone: bezel, low profile, smooth rim, occasional-wear expectations.
- Easy maintenance: plain band, bezel solitaire, simple prongs.
- Maximum visual size: halo, elongated stone, marquise, oval, pear, tapered shank.
- Best resizability: plain lower shank, half-eternity, simple solitaire.

## Stone And Setting Pairing Matrix

- Round diamond: prong, bezel, halo, tension, flush for small stones.
- Oval diamond/sapphire: prong, bezel, halo, east-west, north-south.
- Pear: V-prong plus side prongs, bezel, halo.
- Marquise: V-prongs at both tips, bezel, halo.
- Princess: V-prongs or protective corner setting, channel, bezel.
- Emerald cut: four prongs at corners, bezel, three-stone with baguettes.
- Asscher: prongs, bezel, Art Deco halo.
- Cushion: prongs, bezel, cushion halo, vintage settings.
- Cabochon opal/moonstone/turquoise: bezel, low profile, smooth ring.
- Emerald gemstone: protective prongs or bezel, avoid harsh cleaning assumptions.
- Melee diamonds: pave, bead, channel, flush, shared prong depending on location.

## Final Design Review Questions

- What is the first thing the eye should notice?
- Does the side view support the same design story as the top view?
- Is the ring beautiful only in a render, or also wearable on a hand?
- Where will the ring wear down first?
- How will it be cleaned?
- How will it be repaired?
- Can it be resized?
- What happens if the wearer catches it on fabric?
- Are all pointed stones protected?
- Are all fragile stones treated as fragile?
- Is there enough metal left after stone setting and polishing?
- Does the manufacturing path match the design's delicacy?

## Terminology And Output Validation

Before finalizing an answer, check:

- Did the response distinguish stone shape, cut style, cut quality, carat weight, and face-up size?
- Did the response distinguish karat from carat?
- Did the response name anatomy terms correctly: shank, head, setting, gallery, bridge, shoulders, prongs, bezel, seat, and sizing area?
- Did the response avoid vague style-only language?
- Does the use case match the ring: daily, engagement, wedding, fashion, signet, cocktail, memorial, or occasional?
- Are pointed or cornered stones protected?
- Are soft or brittle stones treated as fragile?
- Are maintenance expectations realistic for pave, micro-pave, halos, clusters, filigree, and eternity bands?
- Can the ring be resized, or is the limitation explicit?
- Is the side profile described clearly enough for a designer, CAD modeller, or renderer?
- Are open questions and assumptions separated from confirmed requirements?
