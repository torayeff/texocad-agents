# Ring Anatomy And Terminology

## Primary Structure

- Ring: jewellery worn around a finger. Can be a plain band, a mounted stone ring, or a sculptural object.
- Band: a complete ring, often without a raised head. In casual language "band" may also mean the circular part of any ring.
- Shank: the circular structural part that wraps around the finger and supports any head or setting.
- Head: the stone-holding assembly on top of the shank. May be a separate soldered component or integrated into the shank.
- Setting: the method and metalwork used to secure stones. Sometimes used interchangeably with head.
- Mounting: the metal ring before stones are set.
- Shoulder: upper shank area rising toward or meeting the head.
- Sizing area: usually the plain lower section of shank reserved for future resizing.
- Bridge: lower support crossing beneath the head, often visible from the side above the finger.
- Gallery: side area beneath the stone and above the bridge. Can be open, closed, decorative, or structural.
- Undergallery: decorative or structural pattern under the centre stone, often visible from side or underside.
- Basket: a setting structure with vertical prongs and horizontal rails, resembling a small basket.
- Peg head: a separate head with a peg inserted into the shank; efficient but can look less integrated.
- Integrated head: head and shank flow into one another as a continuous design.
- Rail: horizontal metal support in a gallery, channel, basket, or stone row.
- Bearing or seat: cut surface where a gem rests in the setting.
- Girdle support: metal contact around the widest outline of the stone.

## Stone Anatomy Terms Used In Ring Design

- Table: flat top facet of a faceted gemstone.
- Crown: upper part of a faceted stone above the girdle.
- Girdle: widest perimeter edge of the stone; the usual contact point for prongs, bezels, and channels.
- Pavilion: lower part of the stone below the girdle.
- Culet: bottom point or small flat facet of the pavilion.
- Facet: flat polished surface on a cut stone.
- Cabochon: domed, unfaceted stone with a flat or gently curved base.
- Calibrated stone: stone cut to standard commercial dimensions.
- Freeform stone: irregular or custom outline requiring a custom setting.
- Melee: small accent diamonds or gems, often under about 0.2 ct each.
- Centre stone: dominant main stone.
- Side stones: larger stones flanking the centre stone.
- Accent stones: small decorative stones on shank, halo, gallery, or shoulders.

## Setting Hardware Terms

- Prong: metal claw that holds a stone at or just above the girdle.
- Claw: another term for prong, often used for finer pointed prongs.
- Shared prong: one prong secures adjacent stones in a row.
- V-prong: V-shaped prong used to protect pointed tips on pear, marquise, heart, and princess stones.
- Tab prong: flatter, wider prong with a tab-like top.
- Button prong: rounded prong tip.
- Bezel wall: continuous or partial metal rim around a stone.
- Bezel lip: upper edge of bezel metal pushed over the stone.
- Half bezel: bezel protecting two opposing sides while leaving other sides open.
- Tube setting: round bezel-like tube used for small stones.
- Channel wall: side wall holding stones in a channel setting.
- Bar: metal strip separating stones in a bar setting.
- Bead: small raised metal dot pushed over a stone in pave or bead setting.
- Bright cut: polished angled cut around bead-set stones, often with reflective edges.
- Grain: small raised bead of metal used to hold stones, especially in antique-style setting.
- Burnish setting: stone is pressed into a drilled seat and surrounding metal is burnished over the edge.
- Seat depth: how far the stone sits down into the setting.
- Stone clearance: space between pavilion or culet and the finger rail, gallery, or adjacent metal.

## Shank Profiles

Shank profile means cross-section shape.

- Flat: flat outside and inside; crisp, modern, may feel harder at the edges.
- Half-round: domed outside, flatter inside; common classic band profile.
- Court or comfort fit: rounded inside, often rounded outside; easier over knuckles and comfortable for daily wear.
- D-shape: rounded outside with flat or flatter inside; common wedding band profile.
- Knife edge: raised central ridge on the outside; visually slim and directional but can feel sharper.
- Low dome: shallow rounded outside; subtle, easy to stack.
- High dome: pronounced rounded outside; more traditional and weighty.
- Concave: inward-curving outside surface.
- Flat court: flat outside, comfort-rounded inside.
- Euro or European shank: flattened or squared lower outside profile that helps resist spinning.

## Shank Silhouette Terms

Shank silhouette means the top-view path and width behavior.

- Straight shank: consistent width around the finger.
- Tapered shank: narrows toward the head, making the centre stone look larger.
- Reverse taper: wider near the head for support and visual weight.
- Split shank: divides into two or more strands as it approaches the head.
- Bypass shank: two ends curve past or around each other rather than meeting symmetrically.
- Cathedral shank: arched shoulders rise toward the head like buttresses.
- Pinched shank: narrows tightly at the head.
- Twisted shank: two or more strands spiral or cross.
- Crossover shank: strands visibly cross over one another.
- Open shank: gap or negative space in the shank design.
- Wide band: broad shank, often 5 mm or wider.
- Stacking band: narrow band intended to pair with other rings.

## Decorative And Surface Terms

- Milgrain: tiny beaded edge detail, common in vintage and antique-inspired rings.
- Filigree: delicate wire-like openwork.
- Piercing: cut-through open pattern in the metal.
- Engraving: carved or cut surface decoration, including hand engraving or machine engraving.
- Relief: raised sculptural decoration.
- Recessed detail: detail cut below surrounding surface.
- Flush detail: detail level with surface.
- Hammered finish: irregular faceted metal texture.
- Satin finish: soft matte directional finish.
- Brushed finish: linear matte texture.
- Sandblasted finish: fine granular matte finish.
- High polish: mirror-like reflective finish.
- Florentine finish: crosshatched engraved texture.
- Blackened or oxidized: darkened recesses, commonly on silver or treated gold surfaces.
- Two-tone: two metal colors or alloys in one ring.
- Inlay: material set into a recess, such as enamel, stone, wood, shell, or ceramic.
- Enamel: fused glassy colored surface; can chip and may limit resizing.

## Common Ring Feature Terms

- Halo: border of small stones around a centre stone.
- Hidden halo: halo placed below the centre stone, mostly visible from the side.
- Double halo: two concentric halos.
- Side-stone setting: centre stone with adjacent larger stones.
- Trilogy or three-stone: three main stones, often symbolic of past, present, future.
- Eternity: stones continue all the way around the ring.
- Half eternity: stones cover only the top half or visible portion.
- Quarter eternity: stones cover a smaller front segment.
- Solitaire: one primary stone with no major side stones.
- Cluster: multiple stones grouped to read as a larger motif.
- Toi et moi: two primary stones, often contrasting shapes or colors.
- Signet face: flat or raised top panel for engraving, crest, initials, or seal.
- Seal engraving: reverse or intaglio engraving intended to impress wax.
- Flush-fit band: wedding band can sit directly beside engagement ring without a gap.
- Contour band: band shaped to fit around another ring.
- Nesting band: band designed as part of a stack around a centre ring.

## Useful Design Language

- Airy: visible negative space, open gallery, thin rails, exposed stone.
- Architectural: strong geometry, structural arches, stepped forms, clear planes.
- Organic: flowing, asymmetrical, botanical, hand-formed shapes.
- Sculptural: ring mass reads as a three-dimensional object, not only stone support.
- Minimal: reduced ornament, clean metal surfaces, few components.
- Vintage-inspired: milgrain, filigree, engraving, old mine or cushion shapes, warm metal.
- Art Deco: stepped geometry, symmetry, baguettes, emerald cuts, black-white contrast.
- Modern: clean bezels, east-west stones, tension, negative space, broad smooth metal.
- Romantic: soft curves, oval/cushion/pear shapes, halos, floral motifs.
- Heirloom: durable, serviceable, classic proportions, repairable setting choices.
