# Ring Design Operating Frame

Design rings as complete jewellery objects, not just attractive top-view shapes. Every concept should balance visual intent, stone language, wearability, material choice, and production realism.

Build each recommendation in this order mentally:

1. Understand the purpose and wearer context.
2. Choose the ring family, silhouette, and style direction.
3. Define the stone program and setting logic.
4. Validate fit, profile, comfort, lifestyle, stacking, maintenance, and resizing.
5. Validate metal, CAD, casting, stone-seat, polishing, and handoff constraints.
6. Produce the right output: quick concept, design card, image prompt, critique, or CAD/jeweller brief.

## Fast Design Axes

Describe and evaluate rings through these axes:

- Purpose: engagement, wedding band, signet, cocktail, fashion, mourning, heirloom, daily wear, occasional wear.
- Wearer lifestyle: active hands, gloves, desk work, wet work, childcare, medical work, sport, formal occasion.
- Visual mass: delicate, airy, slim, balanced, substantial, architectural, chunky, statement.
- Profile height: low profile, medium profile, high profile, proud stone, flush top.
- Stone program: no stones, solitaire, side stones, halo, cluster, eternity, scattered, toi et moi, birthstones.
- Stone security: exposed prongs, protective bezel, channel, flush, shared prong, bead, tension.
- Shank behavior: straight, tapered, knife edge, comfort fit, split, bypass, cathedral, euro, wide band.
- Manufacturing path: hand fabrication, lost wax casting, direct casting from print, CNC, wax carving, assembled components.
- Resizability: plain sizing area, partial eternity, full eternity, engraved shank, patterned shank, tension ring.
- Maintenance burden: low, moderate, high, requires periodic prong checks, ultrasonic safe or not.

## Core Tradeoffs

- Maximum sparkle often means more exposed stone and more snag risk.
- Maximum protection often means more metal around the stone and less side light.
- Thin delicate bands look elegant but can bend, spin, or wear faster.
- Wide bands feel tighter than narrow bands at the same inside diameter.
- Low settings wear easily but may reduce light, cleaning access, or wedding-band flush fit.
- Full eternity and continuous surface decoration reduce resizability.
- Soft or brittle gems need protective settings and occasional-wear expectations.
- CAD models need extra metal for casting shrinkage, polishing loss, stone setting, and future maintenance.

## Minimum Ring Description

Every useful ring recommendation should specify:

- Ring category and intended use.
- Style direction in construction terms, not only adjectives.
- Centre stone, side stones, accent stones, or no-stone metal program.
- Stone shape, cut style where relevant, approximate size if known, and protection needs.
- Setting type, prong/bezel/channel/flush behavior, and side profile.
- Shank width, silhouette, cross-section, comfort fit, and sizing area.
- Metal type or metal color, finish, texture, and plating if relevant.
- Wearability notes: profile height, snag risk, maintenance, resizing, stacking, and lifestyle fit.
- Open questions or assumptions when the user has not supplied critical data.

## Source Notes

The ring design rules are grounded in these source categories:

- Bench jeweller and jewellery supplier education pages on ring anatomy and settings.
- GIA education material on diamond shapes and cut vocabulary.
- Jewellery CAD and casting guidelines for wall thickness, openings, stone seats, and STL handoff.
- Ring sizing and wedding-band fit guides for width, comfort fit, stacking, and knuckle effects.
- Metal and gemstone durability guides for alloy choice, Mohs hardness, toughness, and skin sensitivity.
